About the project

About PAL Privacy Guide

PAL Privacy Guide is an independent educational publication for people who want understandable, evidence-aware guidance about online privacy and security tools.

Purpose

The site turns technical and policy details into practical questions: What problem does a tool solve? What trust does it introduce? What evidence supports its claims? What does it cost now and at renewal? How can a person leave? We aim to make limitations as visible as benefits.

Editorial position

PAL Privacy Guide is merchant-independent. It is not operated by Proton, does not speak for Proton, and is not currently approved by or partnered with Proton or another privacy-tool vendor. There are no testimonials, sponsored rankings, affiliate links, advertising trackers, or conversion tags in this preview.

Who runs it

This is a small, operator-led information project rather than a large newsroom or security consultancy. That scope is stated openly: published work should show its sources, dates, testing limits, and corrections. It should not imply credentials, staff, laboratory capacity, or first-hand testing that the project does not have.

Corrections and contact

For factual corrections, source suggestions, accessibility issues, or privacy questions about this preview, use the public GitHub Issues channel for the PAL Privacy Guide repository. GitHub Issues are public and require a GitHub account to post. Do not post passwords, payment information, identity documents, private addresses, account credentials, or other sensitive personal information. This channel is not emergency support, individualized legal advice, or third-party product support.

Corrections standard

A correction request should identify the page, disputed statement, and supporting source. Material confirmed errors will be corrected with a visible date or note when appropriate. Disagreement with an editorial conclusion does not by itself establish a factual error.

Commercial transparency

Read the current Affiliate Disclosure and the methodology in How We Evaluate Tools. Commercial activity, if ever introduced, requires separate authorization and disclosure.
