Editorial method

How we evaluate privacy tools

Our method separates evidence from marketing, identifies trade-offs, and avoids pretending that one ranking fits every person.

1. Define the use case

We begin with the job a reader needs done: protecting traffic on an untrusted network, reducing IP exposure, securing messages, managing credentials, or limiting data collection. We state the threat model and exclude problems the tool cannot reasonably solve.

2. Examine the organization

We look for clear ownership, operating entities, relevant jurisdictions, leadership accountability, business model, material acquisitions, and disclosed conflicts. A familiar brand is not a substitute for this information.

3. Map data handling

We compare product behavior with the privacy policy: data collected, purposes, retention, processors, disclosures, deletion controls, and choices available without an account. We distinguish content, identifiers, payment records, diagnostics, and connection metadata.

4. Assess security evidence

We consider protocols and cryptography in context, client update mechanisms, secure defaults, leak protections, public technical documentation, vulnerability reporting, independent audits, source availability, and responses to past incidents. Audit scope and date matter; an audit is a snapshot, not a permanent guarantee.

5. Test usability and reliability

When hands-on testing is available, we document device, software version, network, region, test date, and limitations. We examine installation, defaults, accessibility, connection recovery, account deletion, support, and whether protective features behave understandably. Performance results are treated as time- and location-specific.

6. Review cost and exit

We record initial and renewal prices, plan duration, refund conditions, payment requirements, device limits, cancellation flow, export options, and deletion. “Free” is evaluated for data trade-offs, restrictions, upsells, trials, and billing transitions.

Evidence and scoring rules

Primary evidence first

Product documentation, policies, audit reports, source repositories, regulator or court records, and our documented tests take priority over affiliate pages or unsourced summaries.

Dates and uncertainty

Claims carry an “as of” date where change is likely. Missing evidence is labeled unknown; it is not converted into a favorable assumption.

No universal winner

We may describe best fits for defined needs, but we do not publish unsupported “best overall” claims or present small test differences as universal facts.

Commercial independence

Editorial conclusions must be written before any commercial link is added. Compensation, if introduced later, would not purchase a rating, suppress limitations, or guarantee coverage. Any page containing a compensated link would identify it near the link and follow the site-wide Affiliate Disclosure. At present, this site has no affiliate relationships or affiliate links.

Corrections

Material factual errors should be corrected promptly, with the change date and nature of the correction recorded on the affected page. Readers can use the contact purpose described on our About & Contact page to report a possible error and include a source.
