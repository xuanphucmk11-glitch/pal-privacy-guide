A practical privacy baseline

  1. Protect your accounts. Use a password manager, unique passwords, and multi-factor authentication. Prefer phishing-resistant methods when available.
  2. Keep devices current. Install security updates, remove unused apps and extensions, and review permissions periodically.
  3. Choose communications deliberately. Encryption can protect message contents, but metadata, recipients, backups, and endpoints may still reveal information.
  4. Decide whether a VPN fits. A VPN can protect traffic on the path to its server and hide your public IP from destination sites, but it transfers some trust to the VPN provider.
  5. Reduce collection. Share less, reject unnecessary permissions, use privacy-respecting defaults, and delete data you no longer need.

What we will not promise

No single service makes a person anonymous, prevents every breach, or replaces secure devices and careful behavior. Privacy depends on your situation: who might seek your information, what they can access, and what consequences matter to you. Our guides identify assumptions and limitations rather than offering guarantees.
