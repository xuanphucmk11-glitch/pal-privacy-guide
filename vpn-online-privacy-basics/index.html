VPN and online privacy basics

A VPN is a useful network tool, not an invisibility cloak. The right decision starts with a clear picture of what changes—and what does not.

What a VPN changes

Without a VPN, your device normally connects to a destination through your internet provider. HTTPS can encrypt the content exchanged with a properly configured website, while network observers may still infer destinations and timing. A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and the VPN server. The destination sees the VPN server's public IP address instead of the IP assigned to your connection.

This can be valuable on networks you do not control, for reducing routine exposure of your public IP to sites, and for changing which network operator can observe destination-level traffic patterns.

Trust moves; it does not disappear

The VPN operator becomes part of the trust path. Depending on the service design and connection, it may be able to observe account information, connection metadata, DNS requests, or unencrypted traffic. Marketing labels such as “no logs” are not enough by themselves. Look for precise policies, technical design, independent assessments, incident history, ownership, and the legal environment.

Before choosing a service

  • Write down the situation you are trying to improve and the likely observer or threat.
  • Check supported devices, update practices, kill-switch behavior, DNS handling, and protocol options.
  • Read the privacy policy for what is collected, why, retention periods, sharing, deletion, and account requirements.
  • Look for verifiable evidence: open-source clients, reproducible builds where available, independent audits with scope and date, and transparent incident responses.
  • Check renewal pricing, refund rules, payment options, simultaneous-device limits, and how to export or delete account data.

A broader baseline matters more

Use unique passwords and multi-factor authentication; update operating systems, browsers, routers, and apps; encrypt device storage; back up important data; limit extensions and app permissions; review account recovery methods; and treat unexpected prompts as suspicious. A VPN complements these controls—it does not replace them.

Scope note: this guide is general education, not individualized security, legal, or safety advice. People facing stalking, targeted surveillance, domestic abuse, or high-risk professional threats should seek specialist support appropriate to their location.
